# Care Network Model

Evernest collaborative care is centered on the person receiving care. The care network exists to preserve operational continuity around that person without introducing organizational account structures or enterprise administration.

## Care Subject

The care subject is the individual receiving care and the canonical operational center.

All operational continuity should answer:

- who this action was for
- who performed it
- what role they held
- when it happened
- what operational context it belongs to

The care subject remains the center of operational continuity.

## Care Circle

The care circle is the lightweight collaborative network surrounding a care subject. It includes family, caregivers, supporters, aides, and provider contacts who contribute operational context.

The care circle is not an organization, workspace hierarchy, or enterprise account model. It is a care-centered collaboration boundary.

## Care Actor

A care actor is any participant contributing operational context.

Examples:

- family
- caregiver
- spouse
- aide
- provider
- specialist
- supporter

Actors should be represented through stable identity, display name, relationship, and role. Actor attribution should travel with operational events so replay remains understandable.

## Relationship Philosophy

Relationships explain why a person participates in care. Roles explain what kind of operational contribution they make.

Initial roles:

- Primary Caregiver
- Family Member
- Provider
- Supporter

These are lightweight continuity labels, not enterprise RBAC.

## Collaborative Continuity Principles

- Operational events belong to the care subject.
- Actions are attributed to care actors.
- Timeline and summary projections should preserve actor context.
- Shared visibility should be explicit and understandable.
- Collaboration should reduce caregiver confusion, not create administration overhead.

## Operational Ownership

The event history is the operational source of truth. Care actors create events, but the care subject anchors continuity.

## Attribution Philosophy

Attribution should make care coordination more trustworthy:

- who acted
- under which role
- for which care subject
- in what operational context

Attribution must not become surveillance or blame framing. It exists to support continuity and shared understanding.
