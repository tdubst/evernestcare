# Operational Attention Model

Operational attention is the layer that helps caregivers decide what to review next. It should reduce uncertainty, not increase stress.

## Philosophy

Attention surfaces should prioritize continuity trust:

- show relevant gaps plainly
- explain why something surfaced
- preserve attribution when available
- avoid urgent language unless the product has explicit emergency workflow support

The model is coordination-first. It does not judge care quality or provide clinical advice.

## Caregiver Visibility Priorities

1. Medication confirmation gaps.
2. Missing vitals or recent measurements.
3. Caregiver observations that may need review.
4. Missing follow-up documents after discharge or referral context.
5. Care-circle participation visibility.

## Surfacing Rules

Signals should be:

- small in number
- explainable in one sentence
- linked to timeline context when possible
- timeframe-aware
- derived from projections

Avoid persistent alert banners unless the signal represents a clear operational blocker. Default to calm visibility.

## Continuity Confidence

Continuity confidence grows when recent operational events, artifacts, and actor attribution are visible. The app should show confidence through clarity and completeness rather than scores, grades, or clinical risk labels.

## Non-Goals

- Alarm fatigue.
- Alert spam.
- Anxiety-inducing medical UX.
- Enterprise monitoring dashboards.
- Clinical escalation automation.
